Decisions under uncertainty, modelled honestly.

Bayesian modelling is our specialty. We're running a small pilot with a few organisations that have a real question and imperfect data.

What it is

Start from what you already know, update it with the data you observe, and get back not a single number but a full picture of what is likely — and how sure you can be. It works well with small or messy data, lets expert knowledge count, and turns uncertainty into something you can act on.

prior × data → posterior

Three quick examples

Weather

History It rains on 30% of October days here.

Today The pressure dropped sharply this morning.

before30%
after75%

→ 75% chance of rain this afternoon.

Wildfire

History A fire starts on about 2 summer days in 100 in this valley.

Today Three weeks without rain, and strong winds forecast.

before2%
after20%

→ 1 chance in 5 of a fire tomorrow — ten times the usual.

Epidemiology

History 1 person in 100 has the disease.

Today You test positive, with a test that is right 90% of the time.

before1%
after8%

→ Only an 8% chance you're actually ill: most positives come from the 99 healthy people.

Numbers are illustrative.

Where it helps

  1. Experiments with little dataA/B tests, pilots, clinical or field trials: get "there's an 87% chance B is better" instead of a p-value, and decide earlier.
  2. Forecasting with honest rangesDemand, revenue, staffing across many stores or products — sparse series borrow strength from each other, and every forecast comes with its uncertainty.
  3. Risk where events are rareFailure rates, fraud, incidents: combine scarce observations with expert judgement to estimate risks you can't measure directly.
