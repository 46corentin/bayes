BAYES / PILOT

We help you take action to mitigate risks.

Bayesian modelling is our specialty. We're running a small pilot with a few organisations that have a real question and imperfect data.

What it is

Start from what you already know, update it with the data you observe, and get back not a single number but a full picture of what is likely — and how sure you can be. It works well with small or messy data, lets expert knowledge count, and turns uncertainty into something you can act on.

prior × data → posterior

Two quick examples

Weather

History It rains on 30% of October days here.

Today The pressure dropped sharply this morning.

before30%
after75%

→ 75% chance of rain this afternoon.

Wildfire

History A fire starts on about 2 summer days in 100 in this valley.

Today Three weeks without rain, and strong winds forecast.

before2%
after20%

→ 1 chance in 5 of a fire tomorrow — ten times the usual.

Numbers are illustrative.

From forecast to action

We don't stop at the probability. We build dynamic models that recommend what to do when a risk rises — and update the plan as new data comes in, until the risk is back to a level you accept.

Wildfire → forest management

Risk Fire risk in the valley has risen to 20%.

Plan Clear undergrowth on the driest slopes, open two firebreaks, add patrols on windy days.

now20%
with plan4%

→ Back under the 5% you set as acceptable.

Rain → harvest plan

Risk Rain this afternoon is now 75% likely; 40% of the crop is still in the field.

Plan Harvest the ripest plots this morning, move crews from the sheltered plots, cover what's already cut.

now40%
with plan8%

→ Crop exposed to rain drops from 40% to 8%.

What about you?

Whatever your field, you may recognise yourself here:

  1. You have data, but not enough to be sure.A few months of history, a small sample, a pilot that just started.
  2. Your experts know things the numbers don't.We turn that experience into something the model can use.
  3. You need a range, not a single number.To plan, budget or decide with your eyes open.
  4. Something rare could cost you a lot.And you'd like to see it coming, and know what to do.

No technical background needed — just a question and the data you have, however imperfect.
